# 3.4 Representation and similarity

Code: `analysis/keystroke_io.py` (loading and cleaning), `analysis/similarity.py`
(windows, features, distances, evaluation). Reproduce everything with

```bash
python analysis/similarity.py data/*.csv          # ~40 s, writes results/similarity/
python tests/test_similarity.py                   # sanity checks of the distances
```

## Data used

| Problem found in `data/` | What the loader does |

| every session starts with the test phrase `.tie5Roanl` typed 10 times | removed: it is fixed text, not free text, and would make sessions look alike |

After cleaning: 4 participants, 11 sessions, about 24,000 keystrokes. Digraph latencies
are only computed **inside a message** and when the press–press time is **≤ 1 s** (98 %
of the transitions); longer gaps are reading or thinking, not rhythm. Keys are identified
by `key_code` (physical key), so `r` and `R` are the same key.

## Representation of a window

A sample is a **window of n consecutive keystrokes** of one person in one session
(non-overlapping, crossing message boundaries since messages are often shorter than n).
It is described in two ways:

1. **Global vector (9 values):** median and IQR of hold time, median and IQR of
   press–press latency (DD), median release–press latency (UD), overlap rate (UD < 0),
   pause rate, Backspace rate, Shift/CapsLock rate.
2. **n-graph profile:** median log-duration of each frequent **unigram** (hold time of a
   key) and **digraph** (DD latency). Only items typed **≥ 10 times by every participant**
   are kept: 27 keys + 88 digraphs. Items used by only one person are excluded on purpose:
   their mere presence would identify the person, but it says nothing about rhythm.

Durations are taken in **log**: typing times are right-skewed, and a difference of logs is
a ratio, so "20 % slower" means the same for a 80 ms hold and a 300 ms latency.

## Distances compared

| Name | Definition | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| `global` | mean \|z_a − z_b\| over the 9 z-scored global features | coarse, always defined |
| `ngraph` | mean \|log a − log b\| over the n-graphs **both windows share** | ratio of durations, no normalisation |
| `ngraph_z` | same, each n-graph divided by its spread over all windows | every n-graph weighs the same |
| `R` | normalised disorder between the two windows' rankings of shared digraphs (Gunetti & Picardi [5]) | only the *order* of durations, insensitive to speed |
| `A` | 1 − share of shared digraphs whose durations are within a ratio of 1.25 ([5]) | counts "similar" digraphs |
| `R+A` | sum of the two, as in [5] | |
| `quantile` | mean \|q_a − q_b\| over 19 quantiles of log hold and log DD | distance between distributions (1-D Wasserstein) |

n-graph distances need at least **5 shared items**; otherwise the pair is "not comparable".

## How a distance is judged

All pairs of windows are labelled *genuine* (same person) or *impostor*. Genuine pairs are
taken from **different sessions** (the realistic case: the profile was learned another
time); same-session and different-day results are also in `similarity_summary.csv`.

- **AUC** = probability that a genuine pair is closer than an impostor pair (0.5 = chance).
- **EER** = error when the distance threshold makes false accepts = false rejects.
- **1-NN accuracy** = for each window, is the nearest window from *other* sessions
  from the same person? (chance = 25 %).

## Results

Separation vs window length, genuine pairs from other sessions (full table in
`results/similarity/similarity_summary.csv`):

| window (median typing time) | global | ngraph | **ngraph_z** | R | A | R+A | quantile |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 keys (5 s) | 0.77 | 0.85 | **0.88** | 0.65* | 0.73* | 0.74* | 0.88 |
| 50 keys (12 s) | 0.86 | 0.93 | **0.95** | 0.68 | 0.77 | 0.77 | 0.93 |
| 100 keys (29 s) | 0.92 | 0.98 | **0.99** | 0.81 | 0.87 | 0.89 | 0.95 |
| 200 keys (65 s) | 0.96 | 0.995 | **0.999** | 0.92 | 0.94 | 0.97 | 0.96 |
| 400 keys (137 s) | 0.98 | 0.999 | **1.00** | 0.98 | 0.98 | 0.99 | 0.97 |

\* only 14 % of pairs comparable at 25 keys (see below).

EER of `ngraph_z`: 20 % at 25 keys, 11 % at 50, 5 % at 100, 2 % at 200.
1-NN identification with `ngraph_z`: 82 % (25 keys), 95 % (50), 100 % (100 and more).

**Observations**

- **How many keystrokes before two windows can be compared?** Two windows of 25 keys
  share a median of 2 frequent digraphs (only 14 % of pairs share ≥ 5); 50 keys → 8
  (89 % of pairs); 100 keys → 20 (all pairs). Hold times of single keys are shared much
  earlier (11 items at 25 keys), which is why `ngraph`/`ngraph_z` work at 25 keys while
  digraph-only measures (`R`, `A`) do not.
- **Normalising helps.** `ngraph_z` beats `ngraph` at every length: without it, n-graphs
  with naturally large variability (long latencies) dominate the average.
- **Order alone (`R`) is the weakest** on our data: with short windows the ranking of
  ~10–20 noisy medians is unstable. Its advantage (insensitive to a global slow-down,
  e.g. fatigue) does not pay off yet with only 4 people; it catches up at 400 keys.
- **Distributions (`quantile`) are good with very little text** (0.88 at 25 keys) but
  saturate around 0.95–0.97: two people with the same speed have similar distributions.
- **Most discriminative features** (Fisher ratio, windows of 100): median DD latency,
  overlap rate, hold time of `t`, median UD, median hold, holds of `e`, space, `a`, `i`.
  Hold times are more discriminative than individual digraphs, which are noisier
  (few occurrences per window).
- **Sessions of the same person stay close** (heatmap): distances between sessions of one
  person (0.6–0.86) are well below distances between people (1.1–1.4). P02 and P03 are the
  closest pair of people (MDS plot), P01 and P04 are the most distinctive.

**Choice for the detector (3.5):** `ngraph_z` (z-normalised mean absolute log-ratio over
shared unigrams and digraphs), with `quantile` as a fallback when two windows share too
little.

## Limits to keep in mind

- Only **4 participants**, one of them (P02) with 2 sessions recorded on the **same day**;
  P03 and P04 also typed S2 and S3 on the same day. With so few people, AUC close to 1 is
  easy; more impostors will lower it.
- The z-normalisation and the vocabulary use all windows (no labels, but all sessions).
  In 3.5–3.6 they must be computed **from training data only**.
- Conversations were typed in **English**, not French as in the protocol.
- Before sharing data outside the pair, the `key` column must be removed or obfuscated
  (section 7): it contains what was typed.
